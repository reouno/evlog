e061: Does the climate alone make places a body can tell apart?

Experiment report - 2026-09-13 - foundation stage A (#74): a default world and a search of 300 candidates, 20 years each, no plants, no bodies

TL;DR

The climate alone makes places. Of 300 generated worlds, 294 hold at least five habitats of 2%, and 34 pass all four lines of stage A: enough habitats, three of them wider than three lives of travel, 10-50% changing in a year, and a map that stands. 30 of the 34 are 512 cells wide, so the world is 512 from here. Next, stage B puts producers on these worlds, on a slow clock: per cell per step, a 512 world is too slow to search.

1. Question

Before anything lives on it, does the physical world make places a body can tell apart: wide enough that a body cannot average them away, changing with the seasons, and not drifting? This is the cheapest layer of the foundation, so it is searched widely, and it decides the world's size.

  1. At Earth-like ratios the laws make 5 habitats of 2%, without tuning.
  2. The width line decides the size: 256 fails it, 512 passes.
  3. The tilt sets the change.
  4. The map stands, except where lakes are still filling.

2. The world

A torus with generated terrain and sea; its rows run from one latitude to another and back. Every 10 steps the sun heats each cell, the air takes up water by temperature, the wind carries it, and it rains where the air cannot hold it. Each quarter a cell's habitat is read (Figure 1).

Runs. The default world (256, 20 years), then a Latin hypercube of 300 candidates over 14 axes (size 256 or 512, terrain grain, land share, relief, latitude, day, year, sun, tilt, land response, wind, its turn, rain), 20 years each. Seven that did not stand were rerun for 80 years.

  • habitats - of 15, those holding 2% of the cells over the last year.
  • wide - habitats whose median cell lies in a patch 42 cells wide (the largest square inside it).
  • change - cells whose habitat is not the same in all four quarters.
  • stand - agreement of the last year's four maps with the middle year's.

  1. Yes 294 of 300 worlds hold 5 or more habitats of 2%; the default world holds 12.
  2. Yes 90 of 150 worlds of 512 pass the width line, 24 of 150 of 256; the terrain's grain matters as much as the size.
  3. Yes Change follows the tilt (rank correlation 0.66); 95 of 134 worlds tilted 10-30 degrees pass.
  4. No Lakes are not the cause: a short year fails to stand, and all 7 reruns stand at 80 years.

3.1 Width is bought by size and continents

The wide habitats are the deep sea and dry land. A wet or moist land habitat is wide in at most 8 worlds of 300: the rain falls on windward coasts and in belts, so wet land comes in strips. Only at 512 with continents about 128 cells or wider does land hold several habitats a body cannot cross in a life.

3.2 The tilt sets the change; a short year only needs longer to settle

Change fails by excess four times as often as by lack (100 against 25): a strong tilt moves most cells across a temperature band every year. Stand is not drift. It follows the climate updates run, not the years: with 1,000 updates a year or more, 141 of 144 stand. The sea and the ground water need 10,000-20,000 updates to settle.

4. Discussion

The day's length, the rain rate, the wind speed, the relief and the sun's strength hardly change whether a world passes (each 0.13 or less). They change what it is like: passing worlds run from -6 C to 31 C on land and from 26 to 3,700 mm of rain a year. The lines count places; they do not ask for a mild world. Stage B will, through what grows.

The widths matter for stage C too. The wide land is dry land. Wet land is a coast or a belt a few cells wide, so a law about wet and dry ground will be felt by bodies near a coast and averaged away by the rest, unless bodies travel less than the 14 cells assumed here.

What this does not show: the habitats come from our thresholds (5 and 20 C, a third and two thirds of the ground's fill) and quarter means; the sea's depth follows the land's relief, so a low relief makes a shallow sea; there is no ice, snow or erosion.

5. Conclusion and next step

Stage A passes. The world is 512 with continents of about 128 cells or more, a tilt of 10-30 degrees, and a spin-up of about 20,000 climate updates before anything is judged. The 34 passing worlds are stage B's starting set. Its first problem is compute: e059's world cost 3 ms a step at 128, which is 48 ms at 512, so the producers go on a slow clock like the climate.
