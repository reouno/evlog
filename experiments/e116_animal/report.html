e116: what does a world at the size of an animal cost?

Experiment report - 2026-11-11 - a speed prototype for #135: three benches, two populations, four recordings, seed 1

TL;DR

A decision costs about 1 us, but the placeholder mind decides far more often than hoped: 2,366 times an animal-day in the first population, 7,583 in the second, against 27-274 hoped. Almost all of the second's decisions are the one-second tick taken beside a body of another size. 1e4 animals cost 2.6-5.6 core-hours a year. Next: pace that tick by how fast the other closes, and find why a decision's cost grows with N.

1. Question

e115's body was some 300,000 small animals and a step 44 minutes, so nothing on the screen was an animal. #135 rebuilds the world at an animal's size: one body one animal from 1 kg, grass a field, time continuous. This crate holds no law to keep; it asks what that size costs and how it looks.

  1. Cheap decisions. Decisions paced by events cost 1e4-1e5 an animal-year at a few us each: 1e4 animals run a year in well under a core-hour.
  2. Flat in N. At a fixed density, the cost an animal-year is flat in N.
  3. Animals on screen. Animals at their own size over 5 m ground read as animals on a landscape.

2. The world

An island with a hill and trees on 5 m cells; grass regrows, worked out when read. An animal has a mass (1-200 kg), energy and a linear controller from its senses to a velocity and a bite, which kills at contact anything under 2/3 its mass. It decides at its own time from one queue (Figure 1).

Runs. Ubuntu, one thread each, seed 1. Benches: 1k, 10k and 30k fixed animals at 400 a km2, bites and deaths off, 60 days (10k and 30k stopped after 6 days and 1). live: 300 founders on 9.6 km2 of land for two years. live2: the same after the second look's fixes, one year. Four viewer recordings. Every simulated day we record:

  • decisions an animal-day - the cost driver; 27-274 was hoped.
  • close share - decisions taken as the one-second tick.
  • us a decision - wall time of one decision.
  • notices a decision - other animals whose next decision it moves.
  • animal-years a core-hour - what one core affords.

  2. No hypothesis 2: from 1k to 30k at one density a decision rises from 1.65 to 5.5 us, as notices rise 0.3 to 1.6.

No gallery of bodies: every animal is the same placeholder shape, a trunk on four legs.

3.1 A decision is cheap, but its price grows with N

The count an animal-day barely moves with N, but a bench is a world of endless chases: with bites off, a hunter follows its prey at a tick a second for 60 days, so 37-51% of all decisions are ticks, and no bench number is the cost of a living world. What grows is the price of each decision, with the notices it sends.

3.2 A population holds; hunters do not

Both islands hold a population. live peaks at 1,696 on day 150 and then holds at 926-1,470; live2, whose animals crop while walking and less from a short sward, holds at 186-291 until day 150 and rises to 394. In both, hunters fall to 1-2 within a year: a placeholder mind that does not search does not live on flesh.

3.3 The second look decides three times as often, nearly all of it the one-second tick

The second look's fixes tripled the count, and not through its short walking legs: 97% of live2's decisions are ticks, against 5% in live. The tick fires beside any body 1.5 times heavier or a third lighter, hunter or not, and the second look lengthened bodies (0.25 to 0.4 m kg^-1/3), widening that radius. Whether that or the new grazing gait does it is not separated.

4. Discussion

The price of a decision is not the problem; how often it is taken is. At these rates a world of 1e4 animals for 300 years costs 771 (live) to 1,669 (live2) core-hours on one thread. Most of the excess is the fixed one-second tick, taken beside a grazer of another size as beside a hunter. A tick paced by the time the other takes to close would leave grazers side by side on their own clocks.

Hypothesis 2 fails at the notices, which grow with N at a fixed density. Why is not known; the meeting test on 50 m buckets is the place to look before the next crate.

What this does not show: the cost of a mind that searches or learns, of climate or water, or of several threads. live and live2 are one seed each.

5. Conclusion and next step

No for the count, no for the scaling, partly for the screen. A decision is cheap, so the world at an animal's size is affordable only if the mind decides less often: pace the near tick by how fast a threat closes, find why notices grow with N, and plan for several threads.
