e103: producers that evolve, and the small eaters

Experiment report - 2026-09-26 - the redesign's second rung (#116): 256 random producer genomes on e102's ground, two runs of 300 years

TL;DR

Producers with a genome, tissue chemistry, seeds, fire and leaf-eaters were sown on the ground of rung 1. The world stands for 300 years and the founders sort by place (3.3 and 5.5 groups). But the producers never evolve: in both runs no genotype born after the sowing holds any biomass, because a stand never dies of age and a mutant never finds a free slot. The eaters, which do turn over, evolve and follow the producers' keys. Next: generations inside a stand.

1. Question

Do many producer forms arise, hold by place and keep changing, while the world stands? If producers alone do not diversify over this ground, the redesign's premise fails early. Five lines, set before the runs:

  1. The ledgers close - water, A, B and the living's matter, to 1e-9.
  2. The world stands - half the land and a fifth of the sea held, biomass steady.
  3. Forms hold by place - 5 effective groups, group against place NMI 0.2.
  4. They keep changing - the leader changes 3 times in 100 years; a late group holds 1%.
  5. The living change the ground - evaporation by 10%, the soil's A:B map.

2. The world

A producer is a stand of one genotype a cell (up to 4 a cell). Genes of 16 bases set each organ's A and B, the height, roots, seeds, a defence compound and its key, and plastic responses; small eaters carry detox keys.

Runs. c1225, sown in year 10, run to year 310, two seeds of the living; about 1.8 hours each on 5 threads. Measured:

  • Groups - genotypes grouped by 21 scaled traits (fixed cut), Hill number over biomass.
  • NMI - how much a cell's leading group says about its place (0 none, 1 all).
  • Born after sowing - biomass of genotypes made by mutation.
  • Key distance - bits between a compound and the nearest eater key.

3.1 The world stands and sorts its founders by place

Of 256 founders, 115-121 remain at year 310. The land fills in about a century and then holds; the groups fall from 16-35 in the first year to 4.3 by year 100 as the founders sort, and each keeps to its region.

3.2 Only the eaters evolve

About 116,000 producer mutants were made in each run, and all stayed in seed banks or died as seedlings. Eaters move and replace each other in a cell every season, and 93-96% of their biomass is new genotypes. Their keys come within 1 bit of the producers' compounds, or, in run 2, they carry none and eat the 68% of biomass that is undefended.

3.3 The living remake the soil, not the water

Half of the land's evaporation now passes through leaves, but the shade takes as much from the soil's own, and the runoff stays at 10%. The soil's chemistry is the producers' now: its A:B map correlates only 0.31-0.35 with the bare ground's.

4. Discussion

The failure is one law, not the premise. A stand here is immortal: its tissue turns over but the stand never dies of age, so a slot frees only after a disaster, and the parent's own seeds, nine times the mutant's share, win it back. A real stand is a population whose plants die and are replaced by seedlings every generation, which is how a mutant or an immigrant enters. The eaters show the same machinery works when turnover exists.

What held without evolution is worth keeping: a world that stands for 300 years, forms that keep to places, soils made by the living, eaters that track keys. The two runs share 26% of their biomass in matching groups: which founders win is already contingent.

5. Conclusion and next step

The world stands, but rung 2's question is not yet answered: producers sort, they do not evolve. Next (e104): generations inside a stand - plants die at a lifespan the genome sets, paid for in tissue, and the cell's seed bank recruits into the space - on the same world, judged by the same five lines.
