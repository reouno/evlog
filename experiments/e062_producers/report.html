e062: Do grass, wood and algae each hold a place of their own?

Experiment report - 2026-09-13 - foundation stage B (#75): three pilots and 96 candidates on six stage-A worlds, no bodies

TL;DR

Sometimes. Of 96 candidates (16 draws of growth rates and lightning on six climates), 9 pass every line of stage B, on four worlds, and no draw passes on more than two. Algae always hold the water; grass and wood split the land only when wood needs 3-12 times the ground grass needs. The hot dry world loses its wood and the cold world never burns. Stage C takes one draw, d11, on the very wet and the cool world.

1. Question

A consumer can only specialise on foods that differ. Before any body lives here, do several producers hold places of their own on a stage-A world, and last? Grass, wood and algae share one growth law and differ in material facts: most of a tree is trunk, and algae live in water.

  1. Each producer holds 5% of the plant matter and wins a habitat on most of six worlds.
  2. Fire's reach follows the fuel's regrowth more than the lightning.
  3. The land keeps 95% of its matter.
  4. A candidate takes under 10 minutes at 512 on one core.

2. The world

e061's climate, unchanged, on six of its passing worlds, from hot and wet to cold. Every 10 steps each cell grows its producers out of its soil by light, warmth and water, and loses them to death, rot and fire on dry ground; the smoke falls with the rain (Figure 1).

Runs. The climate is spun up for 20,000 updates, then the producers run for at least 10 years and 200,000 steps. Pilots at the defaults on three worlds, then 16 Latin-hypercube draws of the three growth rates and the strike chance, each on all six worlds.

  • share - a producer's part of the world's standing plant matter, last year.
  • larger part - habitats (2% of the cells, a tenth of the mean plant matter) where a producer is half the standing matter.
  • alive - its least share at the end of any year.
  • burnt - land burnt a year, second half of the run.
  • land matter - the land's matter at the end over the start.
  • need - half / (rate x life): the least light x warmth x water a producer stands on.

  1. No 9 of 96 candidates pass, on 4 of 6 worlds; no draw passes on more than 2.
  2. Partly Big fires only in the hot wet and the cool world (slopes 0.24 and 0.34); elsewhere the land burnt follows the lightning.
  3. Mostly 84 of 96 keep 95%; the loss follows the land burnt (rank -0.92), up to 1.2% a year.
  4. Yes 8.1 minutes a candidate on the Mac, nine at once.

3.1 Algae hold the water; the ratio of needs splits the land

Grass's part of the land follows how much better ground wood needs than grass (rank 0.90). Under a ratio of about 3, wood fills every habitat it can stand in; over about 12, grass takes the land and wood keeps the wettest belt. Both win a habitat in 25 candidates, all between 2.6 and 12.3. Algae were the larger part of a water habitat in all 96.

3.2 The worlds at the ends fail for their own reasons

In the hot dry world wood falls under 1% in 12 of 16 draws. In the hot wet world wood holds the wet land and the dry land is nearly bare, so grass wins no habitat with enough matter in 12 of 16. The cold world burns under 1% of its land in every draw.

3.3 Fire burns too little, and its smoke drains the land

All 58 fire failures burn under 1%; no candidate burns more than 11% a year. On four worlds each fire stays a patch of hundreds of cells, so the land burnt follows the lightning. Where dry fuel is continuous in the dry season, one fire burns up to 27,128 cells. Half of what burns goes to the air, which rains mostly on the sea.

4. Discussion

The band is narrow, and it moves with the world. The same draw passes the very wet and the cool world and fails the mild one, where wood's ground is rarer. Laws are the same in every world, so stage C takes one draw and the worlds it passes, not the best draw for each world.

Two things will matter in longer runs. The smoke moves matter from the land to the sea and nothing brings it back. And wood is not settled in every run: in two passing candidates it rose 30-33% between the middle and the last year.

What this does not show. The answers hold for our choices: one growth form, warmth from 5 to 20 C, water as the ground's fill, seeds from four neighbours, fire only on ground under a third full, plants that change neither water nor air, ten years, six worlds, and pass lines that reject a cold world for not burning.

5. Conclusion and next step

Stage B passes on 4 of 6 worlds, in 9 of 96 candidates. Stage C (#76) starts from draw d11 (grass 0.010, wood 0.0017, algae 0.0091, lightning 1.6e-6) on the very wet world c1225 and the cool world c1236, where wood is settled and the land keeps 97-99% of its matter. Before runs longer than stage C's, the land needs a way to get matter back from the sea.
