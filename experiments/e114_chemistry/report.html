e114: does a chemistry of keys and locks part the food?

Experiment report - 2026-11-05 - three gates on the trial world, a pilot on the islands, two more life seeds

TL;DR

The world stands with the chemistry in two seeds of three, once the rot softens the dead, at 63-77% of e113's bodies. Locks are born and pay a little, but never pass 2e-5 of the producers' mass: the bodies hardly eat living plants, so a plant's defence is not paid for. e113's animals lived on litter softened by a rule. Next: why a living plant costs a body more than the dead.

1. Question

In e113 the bodies ate litter, 99.8% of their diet, and one founder's line held the world. The dead were the one food that asked nothing. Instead of giving litter a value, e114 gives all matter a general chemistry: a tissue's bulk carries a lock, and a gut digests it as far as its keys fit.

  1. The world stands with it.
  2. Locks spread: two or more each hold 10% of the producers' mass.
  3. Litter is not one food: bodies differ in keys (read only if 2).
  4. The sea parts by food (read only if 2).

2. The world

e113's world, with the lock behind one switch. A lock is a gene, one point mutation from a compound gene. The plain bond, lock 0, is open to all.

Runs. Three gates on the 16 km trial world (bodies sown in year 15), each revising the design. Then the 64 km islands to year 100, and life seeds 2 and 3 to year 60, each against e113.

  • land - plant mass on land over a world never sown with bodies.
  • bodies - animals alive.
  • locked share - producers' mass on a genotype with a lock.

3.1 The first design took the land; a litter as hard as its tissue fed nobody

Small eaters fed by litter grew sevenfold and grazed the leaves down before any body was sown. Taking them off it saved the land but not the bodies. Keeping the dead apart by what they are made of did not help either. Only a rot that wears the dead soft brought the bodies back.

3.2 On the islands the law's world stands in two seeds of three

Both worlds pass a bottleneck of a few dozen bodies after the sowing. In seed 1 the law's world fell to none and e113's held 33. Locks are born every decade and pay a little: their share is 100-1,000 times that of the same genes where nothing reads a lock. They are still nowhere near 10%.

4. Discussion

The gates found a rule the old world stood on. In e113 dead tissue lost its toughness at death, so litter was the softest food in the world. Given its tissue's toughness, it fed no body. Worn soft by its own rot, it feeds three quarters as many.

A lock is a plant's defence, and a defence pays only when the living are eaten. The bodies eat living plants for under 1% of their diet, so there is almost nothing for a lock to defend against. A second possible reason was not read: a lock gene is most often born from a compound gene, and loses that compound.

The user corrected one revision: the dead were first to be parted by organ (leaf soft, wood hard). They are parted by their make instead, so that a parting can come out of the matter or not.

5. Conclusion and next step

The chemistry stays in the crate, and the soft litter rule is gone. The bodies still eat the dead alone. The next step reads, from the laws' own arithmetic, what a living leaf, stem, root or seed costs a body against the dead. Locks can matter only once living plants are eaten.
