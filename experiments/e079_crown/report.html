e079: can a crown keep its ground wet, and where do stands stand?

Experiment report - 2026-09-19 - #91 step 2: two laws on the producers, seven worlds of c1225 without bodies

TL;DR

Wood that rests in the cold puts stands next to the seasonal lawns: seven to eleven times the stand cells at 20-50 degrees. A crown that cuts its ground's evaporation wets a stand's summer floor from 0.43 to 0.58, and no further: on this climate the land's rain is its own evaporation, so a stronger cut takes the rain and drains the land into the sea. Next: bodies on the half-rest world, where the crown must also halve a body's cooling.

1. Question

A refuge needs a stand that keeps its water when the summer takes the lawn's (e078). Two laws were designed for it as a cycle, and they change the plants, so the plants are read alone first. Before the runs, with both laws on:

  1. Placement: stand cells at 20-50 degrees at least double.
  2. Floor: a stand's summer ground fill there reaches 0.6.
  3. Counterweights: the lawn there keeps half its cells, and its rain falls under a fifth.
  4. Parts: S2 alone places stands, S1 alone wets them.

2. Method

e078's world with two rates on the plants' side; both 0 reads e078's world file unchanged.

Runs. Each world builds 17 years of plants on c1225, then runs two years with no bodies: the control, each law alone, and four pairs. Seven runs, one core each, 6 minutes.

  • Stand / lawn - land cell with wood of 1 or more / under 0.1.
  • Mid - 20-50 degrees, both hemispheres.
  • Summer fill - the ground's mean fill in the hemisphere's summer quarter.
  • Lawn's rain - on the control's mid lawn cells.

3.1 Resting wood puts stands beside the lawns

At a full rest a trunk grows and dies only while warm, so warmth cancels out of where it settles and the stands reach the cold south. A half rest keeps most of the mid lawn.

3.2 The floor gets wet only by taking the rain

At rate 1 the land keeps its water and turns it over more slowly. Past 1, full stands shed their rain to the sea (a third of the land at full fill at rate 2) and the lawn dries.

4. Discussion

The design assumed the water a crown saves stays under it. Here it is the rain's source: the wind moves 0.1 cells an update, so the land's air is the land's own evaporation. That is what caps the floor near 0.58.

Real forests send water up through their leaves as well as keeping their floor shaded. Here plants read the ground's fill and never drink it, so one bucket cannot hold both.

The conditions: c1225's slow wind, one 150 mm bucket, a settled world of 17 years.

5. Conclusion and next step

The plants' half of the refuge holds except its water. A body's summer water in a stand at a 0.58 floor closes only if the crown also halves its cooling (e078's budget), so step 3 turns on S3, on the half-rest world. Both rates stay as arguments, 0 by default.
