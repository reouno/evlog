e053: The band under the clock

Experiment report - 2026-09-12 - e049's moving rain run under e052's clock, seeds 9-12 (#59). Answer: no. Under the band the clock buys no long lives, so no body outlives the rain's going, and walking west reaches 3-5% of the band's speed. Not kept.

TL;DR

e049's moving rain is followed by births, not by walking, because a body lives about 200 steps. e052's clock made lives two to four times longer, so here the two run together: the band at 50 steps a column, with and without the clock, seeds 9-12. Under the band the clock lengthens no lives at all - the deaths are hunger, and a body that takes 0.8 of a turn eats 0.8 as much. Not kept. Next: a band over a richer world.

1. Question

e049 moved the rain across the world and the crowd followed it by being born under it: a body lives about 200 steps, and the band stands over a cell for 1,600. e052's clock, which scales a body's own time with its mass, raised the mean age of the living from 278-739 to 874-1,432 in a world without the band. Walking should pay when the food moves (A) and the body outlives the change (B). This runs A and B together for the first time:

  1. The body walks, not only the lineage: walking west at a quarter of the band's speed or more, and above the control's, on three seeds of four.
  2. The long-lived are the crowd now: the bodies aged 1,000 steps or more drift west at half the band's speed, and there are many of them.
  3. The world stands: every winter floor above 50.
  4. The brain pays where a life is long: learners a larger share than without the clock (run only if (1) or (2) holds).

2. The world

No new law: two arguments of the same code are turned on together. The sky's rain falls on a north-south band a quarter of the world wide that moves west a column every 50 steps, and a body of mass m takes (16 / m)^0.25 turns a world step - 0.78-0.86 for the bodies of this world. Everything a body does waits for its turn.

Runs. Pilots on seed 9, 20,000 steps: the band at 100 and 50 steps a column under the clock, and at 100 without it. The batch: band 50 with and without the clock, seeds 9-12, 100,000 steps, eight at once on the Mac, 20 minutes. The runs without the band in the age chart are e052's, on the same seeds.

  • walking west - sub-cells a body moves west by its own actions less east, per step; the band moves 0.08.
  • under the band - the share of the body-steps on the band's columns (a quarter of the world).
  • drift rate - how far west of its birthplace a body aged 1,000 steps or more stands, over its age (median), in cells a step; the band moves 0.02.
  • mean age - the age of the living, in world steps.
  • intake per gut - matter a gut block takes per step.
  • kills' share, diversity - the flesh of kills in what bodies eat; the diversity number (#42).

3.1 The crowd does not walk after the rain, clock or no clock

Walking west is above the control's on every seed, and that is the whole of the effect: 0.0025-0.0042 sub-cells a step against 0.0019-0.0025, where the band moves 0.08. The bodies that live longest are the ones that could walk, and under the clock they stand nearer their birthplace than the control's (2.8 cells against 3.8 at 1,000-1,999 steps of age). The crowd also falls further behind the band, because it moves less.

3.2 The clock buys no long lives where the deaths are hunger

This is the finding. Without the band the same law doubled to quadrupled the mean age of the living and wear killed for the first time; under the band nothing moves, and no body dies of wear. What kills is hunger, 2.0-3.1 deaths a log interval, and the clock makes it worse: the intake per gut falls by 20-30%, because a body eats on its turns and the ground under the crowd is already thin.

3.3 A world that stands, packed into the band, and hunting

The pair stands better than the band alone: no run died, where e049's faster band fell to 16 bodies and killed one world of eight. Half the crowd's body-steps are spent on the quarter of the land under the rain, and there the clock makes it hunt - three hunter worlds of four against two, the opposite of what the clock did without the band, where it ended hunting on all four seeds.

4. Discussion

A long life turned out to belong to a rich world, not to the law we wrote. The clock only lets a body spread its eating and its paying over more steps; what it lives to depends on what else kills it. Where food renews everywhere, the slowed body is fed and grows old enough to wear out. Where the rain stands on a quarter of the land and the crowd is packed under it, the same body starves as young as before.

So the two conditions we wanted work against each other. The moving food (A) is what keeps this world poor, and a poor world does not grow the long lives (B) that would let a body follow the food itself. A world with both has to be gentler than this one: a wider band, or more sun.

What this does not show: whether a richer world under a moving band grows long lives; why the band and the clock together raise hunting (the crowd is denser and the bodies bigger, but contact was not measured); what a brain would do where lives are long.

5. Conclusion and next step

Not kept: nothing was added, and the combination does not make bodies walk. The finding is about conditions rather than laws - before a law can rest on "bodies live long", the world has to be one where they do. Next for #59: the band over a richer world (more sun, or a band over half the land), and one pilot to ask whether lives lengthen there at all; only then is walking worth asking again.
